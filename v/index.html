Sixteen doors.
One of them opened.

Every front page built for the house, in the order it was built. They are all still standing and every one of them still works — the numbers are the sequence, not a ranking.

These are live pages, not mockups. Every form on them writes a real row into the production waitlist table, so every submit button on every one of them is the real one.

  • 14Live

    Keyhole

    The one that shipped Stopped describing the house and made you spy on it. Headline hard left, the hole on the right, and something behind it that looks back.
  • 01

    First

    The arcade The starting point. A red door at the back of a colonnade, fog to the knees, and the line centred over it.
  • 02

    Depth

    The arcade Same door, more building. The arches recede so the door is at the end of something rather than flat on the page.
  • 03

    Brooklyn

    The brownstone Left the haunted-house set and went outside. A real brownstone, a real fire escape, one doorway lit — a block anybody here would recognise.
  • 04

    Hallway

    The brownstone Closer in, and the light in the doorway pushed until it reads as a hallway you can see down.
  • 05

    Stairs

    The brownstone Headline dropped to the lower third so the stoop is the first thing you look at, with the fog banked across the bottom step.
  • 06

    Editorial

    The brownstone The first one to stop centring. Left-aligned serif with an italic line under it — a magazine opener rather than a poster.
  • 07

    Case file

    The brownstone Type shrunk to spaced caps and pushed to the corner, so it reads as a label on evidence instead of an advert.
  • 08

    Poster

    The brownstone The opposite bet. Type as big as it goes, wall to wall, photograph reduced to a ground.
  • 09

    Original

    The porch The porch arrives: columns, lanterns, a COMING SOON banner, figures either side of the steps. The set the later ones keep coming back to.
  • 10

    Original poster

    The porch The poster type from 08 put on the porch from 09, to find out which of the two was doing the work.
  • 11

    Drawn

    The porch The same porch as illustration rather than photograph. Warmer, and markedly less frightening — which was the useful thing to learn.
  • 12

    Photo

    The brownstone Graded cold and blue instead of amber. Reads like a police photograph, which is a different kind of wrong to the one we wanted.
  • 13

    Split

    The porch Fog pulled back so the whole set is visible at once. Clearer — and proof that clearer was the wrong direction. 14 came next.
  • 15

    Living door

    Broke the format A portrait card instead of a full-bleed hero, with the claim underneath it. Built for a phone screen and for a feed.
  • 16

    The list

    Broke the format The only one that argues instead of atmospheres. Names come off the list first — the page is the signup, not a trailer for one.

Fourteen is what the live page is built from. The rest stay up because a version you can still open is worth more than a screenshot of one, and because the case for 14 only makes sense next to what it beat.
